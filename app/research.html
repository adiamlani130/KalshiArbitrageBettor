Case studies

The three studies this project started with, rebuilt on a full data pull. Each pairs Kalshi prices with real scores. Load one of the strategies below, then change anything and see what happens.

Tennis · ATP

Does winning set 1 decide the match?

78.9%

Set-1 winners won 78.9% of 2,145 best-of-3 matches, not the 88% of the old stat, and Kalshi had already priced it in. Betting them lost 2.2%. Fading them at −233 to −400 made +3.9%, but only +22% in the months it was found and −19% in the months before.

NBA · games

Back the favorite who is losing at halftime?

+9.5%

Pre-game favorites down 10+ at halftime returned +9.5% on 161 games, and playoff underdogs +23.8% on 77. Both small samples whose likely range still includes zero. Every pre-game favorite lost 4.2%.

NBA · big deficits

Bet on the comeback?

−47.9%

Backing a team the first time it trails by 20 lost 47.9% over 624 games, and taking more threes made it worse. Long shots are overpriced: the favorite-longshot bias, the one pattern that shows up everywhere.

Where the data comes from. Prices are 1-minute bid/ask candles from Kalshi's public API. The first three datasets add real scores: ATP set results (Jan–Sep 2026) and ESPN NBA play-by-play (2025–26 season). All sports · price paths is prices only, for 14 leagues since August 2025: the price before the game and every 1–2 minutes during it. Game start times come from Kalshi's schedule; tennis and UFC starts are estimated from the burst in trading.

How bets are priced. You buy at the ask 1–5 minutes after the moment, $10 per bet, minus Kalshi's taker fee (7% × contracts × price × (1 − price), rounded up to the cent). "Before the match" tennis prices are the midpoint plus 1¢, because only midpoints were saved for that moment.

Research, not betting advice. Past prices don't guarantee future results, and small samples can look profitable by luck.
